The 9/11 Commission Report

The National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States was created to prepare a full and complete account of the circumstances surrounding the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, including preparedness for and the immediate response to the attacks.

The report also contains recommendations intended to help protect the United States against future terrorist attacks.

The official U.S. Government edition contains the complete report, including the thirteen principal chapters, appendices, notes, illustrations, tables, commission members, and staff information.

Suggested Reading Order

A simple way to work through the material without getting buried.

1
Start with the 9/11 Commission Report.
Read the official government account first. This gives the reader the broad chronology, findings, failures, responses, and recommendations before moving into individual documents.
2
Move to Volume I: Terrorism and U.S. Policy.
This provides background on terrorism policy and the U.S. government's understanding of terrorism before September 11.
3
Read the Afghanistan material.
Volumes II and IV provide historical background on Afghanistan, including the Soviet war and the political history of the country.
4
Move into the hunt for bin Laden.
Volume VI provides background on U.S. special operations and the developing effort to locate and pursue bin Laden.
5
Study the Taliban File.
Volume VII and its later updates document the Taliban's rise, relations with Pakistan, U.S. diplomatic efforts, and the Taliban's relationship with bin Laden.
6
Finish with the later 9/11 document releases.
These records can be used alongside the Commission Report to compare the official narrative with the underlying government records and later-released documentation.

The original institutions and collections behind this page.

National Security Archive

The September 11th Sourcebooks are a collection published by the National Security Archive at The George Washington University. The collection brings together primary-source documentation and related historical records concerning terrorism, U.S. policy, Afghanistan, the Taliban, al-Qaeda, and the September 11 attacks.

The original source collection should always be consulted when researching or using any individual document.

9/11 Commission

The National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States was an independent, bipartisan commission established by Congress and the President in 2002. Its final report was released on July 22, 2004.

The official government edition of the report is preserved through the U.S. Government Publishing Office's GovInfo system.
