The 9/11 Commission Report

The principal official U.S. Government report on the September 11 attacks.

Final Report • National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States • July 22, 2004

The 9/11 Commission Report

The National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States was established to create a full and complete account of the circumstances surrounding the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, including preparedness for and the immediate response to the attacks.

The Commission also made recommendations intended to help protect the United States against future terrorist attacks.

The edition provided by the U.S. Government Publishing Office is designated as the official U.S. Government edition of the Commission's final report.

Suggested Reading Order

A simple path through a large collection of historical records.

1
Start with the 9/11 Commission Report.
Begin with the official government report to establish the broad chronology, findings, preparedness issues, immediate response, and recommendations.
2
Read Volume I: Terrorism and U.S. Policy.
Move into the National Security Archive's primary-source material concerning U.S. terrorism policy and earlier terrorist attacks.
3
Read the Afghanistan background.
Volumes II and IV provide historical material concerning Afghanistan, including the Soviet war and the political history of the country.
4
Study the hunt for bin Laden.
Volume VI provides background concerning U.S. special operations and strategy related to the effort to locate and pursue bin Laden.
5
Study the Taliban File.
Volume VII and its later updates document records concerning the Taliban, Pakistan, U.S. diplomatic efforts, Afghanistan, and bin Laden.
6
Finish with the later document releases.
These additional publications can be examined alongside the Commission Report and the original sourcebooks.
