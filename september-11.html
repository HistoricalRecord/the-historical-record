Remembering the human beings behind the history

September 11, 2001 was an attack that unfolded across New York, Virginia and Pennsylvania. It killed thousands of people and profoundly affected survivors, families, responders, workers and communities for decades afterward.

This page is designed as a place to encounter that history through people, evidence, testimony, photographs, sound, video, chronology and remembrance.

The research sources remain available, but the experience itself lives here.

A note about the record

This archive remembers people before statistics.

The National September 11 Memorial & Museum identifies 2,977 people killed as a direct result of the September 11, 2001 attacks. The Memorial's names underwent a multi-year verification and arrangement process before being inscribed at the Memorial.

The Memorial Museum's In Memoriam exhibition presents portraits of the 2,977 people killed in 2001 alongside the six people killed in the February 26, 1993 World Trade Center bombing. The exhibition also incorporates personal photographs, artifacts and recorded remembrances.

Verification principle

Where a fact, photograph, quotation, biography or historical claim has not been independently verified, this archive will say so. Empty space is preferable to an invented historical record.

After the Cameras Left

The Workers

The recovery story includes people whose work began after the immediate emergency had passed.

They entered the ruins too.

Recovery and cleanup involved workers and volunteers performing rescue, recovery, demolition, debris cleanup and related support services for months after September 11.

The World Trade Center Health Program formally recognizes eligible workers and volunteers who performed these activities at designated locations during defined periods.

The historical record should also preserve the experiences of immigrant workers, including undocumented workers, whose stories have sometimes been harder to document because of immigration, employment and health-care barriers.

Recovery workers Construction, demolition, debris removal, transportation, sanitation and related occupations.
Immigrant workers Workers from immigrant communities participated in the enormous cleanup and recovery operation.
Undocumented workers Their experiences, health consequences and struggles for recognition belong in the historical record.
Health consequences The WTC Health Program provides monitoring and treatment for eligible responders and survivors.

A necessary distinction

This archive will not state that every undocumented worker was categorically denied medical care. The documented history is more complicated. Workers experienced differing levels of access to insurance, information, documentation, resources and public programs, while some eventually received treatment through programs established for people affected by 9/11. Individual stories will therefore be presented individually and sourced rather than generalized.

The Continuing Story

The Health Legacy

For many people, September 11 did not end in 2001.

World Trade Center Health Program

The program provides medical monitoring and treatment for eligible responders and survivors affected by 9/11.

Physical illness

The long-term record includes respiratory conditions, other physical illnesses and cancers associated with qualifying exposure and eligibility determinations.

Mental health

The health record also includes psychological consequences and conditions associated with the attacks and recovery work.

Survivors

People who lived, worked or attended school in the disaster area can be part of the survivor population under program rules.

Responders

Rescue, recovery, cleanup and related workers can qualify under specific activity, location and time requirements.

Research

Medical research continues to examine the long-term consequences of 9/11 exposure.

Years After

The Aftermath

The historical story continued through investigation, memorialization, rebuilding, health programs, compensation and the lives of people who survived.

The FBI describes PENTTBOM as the largest investigation in the Bureau's history, involving enormous numbers of investigative leads, interviews and pieces of evidence.
The National September 11 Memorial was created at the World Trade Center site to honor the people killed in the 2001 attacks and the six people killed in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing.
The World Trade Center site underwent a prolonged process of recovery, planning, construction and memorialization.
The World Trade Center Health Program and September 11th Victim Compensation Fund became major parts of the long-term response.
The aftermath is also the story of the people who continued living with loss, illness, memory, survivor's experiences and changing family histories.

Research integrity

This page intentionally separates verified historical information from material that still requires verification. Photographs, quotations, biographies, social-media posts, videos and individual stories should retain provenance and rights information whenever available. Copyright and privacy restrictions should be respected before material is downloaded, republished or transformed.
