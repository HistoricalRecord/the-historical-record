The Memorial

The people whose names are inscribed at the Memorial.

2,977 people

The September 11 attacks killed 2,977 people in New York, at the Pentagon and aboard United Airlines Flight 93.

Their names are permanently inscribed around the two Memorial pools. The National September 11 Memorial & Museum arranged the names using relationships between victims, locations, affiliations, flight crews, first responder agencies and requests from families.

The official Memorial list is used by this archive as the authoritative source for the spelling and ordering of the names.

September 11, 2001: The Day

A chronological record from the first impact through the President's address to the nation that evening.

7:59 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 11 departs Boston

Flight 11 leaves Boston Logan International Airport bound for Los Angeles.

8:14 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 175 departs Boston

Flight 175 leaves Boston Logan for Los Angeles.

8:20 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 77 departs Washington

Flight 77 leaves Washington Dulles International Airport for Los Angeles.

8:42 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 93 departs Newark

Flight 93 leaves Newark International Airport for San Francisco.

8:46:40 a.m.

Flight 11 strikes the North Tower

American Airlines Flight 11 crashes into floors 93 through 99 of the North Tower of the World Trade Center.

8:50 a.m.

The President is informed

President George W. Bush is informed that a plane has struck the World Trade Center while visiting Emma E. Booker Elementary School in Sarasota, Florida.

9:03:11 a.m.

Flight 175 strikes the South Tower

United Airlines Flight 175 crashes into floors 77 through 85 of the South Tower.

9:05 a.m.

The President is told America is under attack

White House Chief of Staff Andrew Card informs President Bush that a second aircraft has struck the World Trade Center.

9:17 a.m.

Nationwide ground stop

The FAA orders a nationwide ground stop, preventing additional aircraft from departing U.S. airports.

9:24 a.m.

Flight 77 is identified as hijacked

FAA personnel receive information indicating that American Airlines Flight 77 has been hijacked.

9:37:46 a.m.

Flight 77 strikes the Pentagon

American Airlines Flight 77 crashes into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia.

9:42 a.m.

Aircraft are ordered to land

The FAA orders all aircraft operating in U.S. airspace to land at the nearest suitable airport.

9:45 a.m.

The White House is evacuated

The White House and other federal buildings are evacuated as authorities respond to the attacks.

9:59 a.m.

The South Tower collapses

The South Tower collapses approximately 56 minutes after being struck by Flight 175.

10:03:11 a.m.

Flight 93 crashes in Pennsylvania

United Airlines Flight 93 crashes into a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after passengers and crew attempt to regain control of the aircraft.

10:28 a.m.

The North Tower collapses

The North Tower collapses approximately 102 minutes after it was struck by Flight 11.

11:00 a.m.

Lower Manhattan is evacuated

Authorities continue the evacuation and emergency response as lower Manhattan remains covered by dust and debris.

12:15 p.m.

U.S. commercial airspace is largely empty

The nationwide grounding of commercial aviation has transformed the normally crowded American skies.

1:00 p.m.

The national response continues

Federal, state and local authorities coordinate emergency response, aviation restrictions, security measures and recovery operations.

1:30 p.m.

The President speaks from Barksdale Air Force Base

President Bush tells reporters that the United States has suffered an apparent terrorist attack and that the country will respond.

2:50 p.m.

The President arrives at Offutt Air Force Base

President Bush is transported to Strategic Air Command headquarters at Offutt Air Force Base in Nebraska.

4:30 p.m.

The President leaves Offutt

President Bush departs Nebraska and returns toward Washington.

7:00 p.m. onward

Washington prepares for the President's address

The President returns to the White House as the country remains under heightened security and the rescue and recovery effort continues at the attack sites.

8:30 p.m.

The President addresses the nation

President Bush speaks from the White House, confirms that the United States has suffered terrorist attacks and announces that the country will pursue those responsible and those who harbor them.

Rescue, Recovery & Health

The historical record continued long after the attacks themselves.

The work continued for months and the health consequences continued for decades.

Firefighters, police officers, paramedics, construction workers, engineers, ironworkers, sanitation workers, volunteers, laborers, cleaners and many other people worked at the World Trade Center site and related locations.

World Trade Center Health Program
The federal program provides medical monitoring and treatment for eligible responders and survivors with certified 9/11-related conditions.

Rescue & recovery

Search, rescue, recovery of human remains, debris removal, structural stabilization, transportation and cleanup continued after the attacks.

Long-term illness

Respiratory illnesses, cancers and other physical and mental health conditions became part of the long-term historical record.

Workers

Immigrant Workers of Ground Zero

Workers whose contributions formed part of the recovery operation.

The recovery workforce was extraordinarily diverse.

Immigrants, including undocumented workers, participated in cleaning, demolition, debris removal and related work after the attacks.

Immigration status, employment arrangements, language barriers and fear could complicate access to information, documentation and assistance.

Immigrant labor Immigrant workers participated in the rescue, recovery, cleaning and rebuilding effort.
Documentation Informal employment arrangements could make documenting work difficult.
Health consequences Immigrant workers were among the populations affected by exposure to the conditions at Ground Zero.
