Portraits & People

The Memorial Museum's In Memoriam exhibition preserves portrait photographs of the people killed on September 11 and in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing.

The portrait record

The National September 11 Memorial & Museum's In Memoriam exhibition contains 2,983 portrait photographs honoring those killed in the September 11, 2001 attacks and the February 26, 1993 World Trade Center bombing.

The Memorial's portrait project has also documented the search for photographs that were initially unavailable. The Museum reported that the portraits of Albert Ogletree and Antonio Dorsey Pratt were ultimately found and installed in 2022.

The complete portrait collection belongs to the Memorial Museum.
This archive links visitors to the institution's portrait collection rather than copying or falsely attributing photographs whose underlying rights and archival records belong elsewhere.

September 11, 2001: The Day

A detailed chronology from the early morning through the President's 8:30 p.m. address.

5:45 a.m. EDT

Hijackers begin moving through airport security

The first group of hijackers begins moving through security at Portland International Jetport and Logan International Airport as the four aircraft involved in the attacks are prepared for their scheduled flights.

6:00 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 11 crew reports for duty

Flight 11 is scheduled to depart Boston for Los Angeles. Passengers and crew begin boarding.

6:30 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 175 crew prepares for departure

Flight 175 is scheduled for the same Boston-to-Los Angeles route.

7:00 a.m.

Flight 77 and Flight 93 passengers begin boarding

American Airlines Flight 77 is scheduled from Washington Dulles to Los Angeles. United Airlines Flight 93 is scheduled from Newark to San Francisco.

7:59 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 11 departs Boston

Flight 11 takes off from Boston Logan International Airport carrying passengers and crew bound for Los Angeles.

8:14 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 175 departs Boston

Flight 175 leaves Logan Airport for Los Angeles.

8:20 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 77 departs Washington Dulles

Flight 77 leaves Dulles International Airport for Los Angeles.

8:24 a.m.

Hijacker communication is transmitted

Air traffic controllers receive an unintended transmission associated with the hijacking of Flight 11.

8:37 a.m.

Boston Center contacts the military

Boston Center contacts the Northeast Air Defense Sector regarding the hijacked aircraft.

8:42 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 93 departs Newark

Flight 93 leaves Newark International Airport for San Francisco, approximately 40 minutes behind schedule.

8:46:40 a.m.

Flight 11 strikes the North Tower

American Airlines Flight 11 strikes the North Tower of the World Trade Center between floors 93 and 99.

8:50 a.m.

President Bush is informed of the first crash

President George W. Bush is informed that an aircraft has struck the World Trade Center while he is visiting Emma E. Booker Elementary School in Sarasota, Florida.

9:02:59 a.m.

Flight 175 strikes the South Tower

United Airlines Flight 175 strikes the South Tower between floors 77 and 85.

9:05 a.m.

The President is informed of the second impact

Andrew Card informs President Bush that a second aircraft has struck the World Trade Center and that the United States is under attack.

9:25 a.m.

The FAA halts all domestic departures

The Federal Aviation Administration issues a nationwide ground stop for departures.

9:30 a.m.

President Bush speaks from Sarasota

The President announces that the United States is dealing with an apparent terrorist attack and orders the full resources of the federal government toward the response.

9:37:46 a.m.

Flight 77 strikes the Pentagon

American Airlines Flight 77 crashes into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia.

9:42 a.m.

The FAA orders aircraft nationwide to land

The FAA orders all airborne civilian aircraft in the United States to land at the nearest suitable airport.

9:59 a.m.

The South Tower collapses

The South Tower of the World Trade Center collapses after burning for less than an hour following the impact of Flight 175.

10:03:11 a.m.

Flight 93 crashes near Shanksville

United Airlines Flight 93 crashes in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after passengers and crew attempt to regain control of the aircraft.

10:15 a.m.

A portion of the Pentagon collapses

A damaged section of the Pentagon collapses as emergency personnel continue working inside and around the building.

10:28 a.m.

The North Tower collapses

The North Tower collapses approximately 102 minutes after Flight 11 struck the building.

11:00 a.m.

New York City remains in emergency response

Emergency services continue rescue, evacuation and recovery efforts throughout Lower Manhattan.

12:16 p.m.

U.S. airspace is effectively cleared

The last known civilian aircraft still airborne under the nationwide grounding order is directed toward landing.

1:00 p.m.

Federal response expands

Federal, state and local authorities continue coordinating the emergency response, evacuation and investigation.

2:51 p.m.

President Bush arrives at Offutt Air Force Base

The President receives continuing intelligence and national-security briefings while the White House coordinates the federal response.

5:20 p.m.

World Trade Center Building 7 collapses

Seven World Trade Center collapses after burning for hours.

7:15 p.m.

Evening federal briefing

Attorney General John Ashcroft, Health and Human Services Secretary Tommy Thompson, Transportation Secretary Norman Mineta and FEMA Director Joe Allbaugh participate in an evening briefing.

8:30 p.m.

The President addresses the nation

President Bush speaks from the Oval Office. He describes the attacks, acknowledges the victims, explains that emergency teams are working in New York and Washington, and tells the country that the federal government has activated its emergency response plans.

8:35 p.m.

The address ends

The President concludes his September 11 address to the nation.

Chronology sources include the National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, federal records, the George W. Bush Presidential Records and the National Archives.

Rescue, Recovery & Cleanup

The emergency continued long after the towers fell.

The work after September 11

Thousands of firefighters, police officers, paramedics, construction workers, engineers, sanitation workers, medical personnel, volunteers, laborers, drivers and other workers participated in rescue, recovery and cleanup operations.

The operation involved searching for survivors and human remains, removing debris, stabilizing structures, cleaning buildings, transporting material and supporting the investigation.

The health consequences of the exposure continued long after the physical recovery operation ended.

The World Trade Center Health Program now provides medical monitoring and treatment for eligible responders and survivors with certified 9/11-related conditions.
WTC Health Program

Immigrant Workers

Immigrant workers were part of the rescue, recovery and cleanup workforce.

Work at Ground Zero

Immigrants participated in cleaning, demolition, debris removal, transportation, food service and other work connected with the recovery operation.

Documentation & Access

Immigration status, informal employment arrangements, language barriers and fear of authorities could complicate workers' ability to document their work and seek assistance.

Health Consequences

Research has documented physical and psychological health problems among immigrant rescue and recovery workers.

Federal Programs

The World Trade Center Health Program and September 11th Victim Compensation Fund have eligibility systems covering qualifying responders, survivors and others.

Victim Compensation Fund

Long-Term Health

The physical and psychological consequences of September 11 continued for decades.

Respiratory Conditions

Exposure to dust, smoke and other substances at the World Trade Center site has been associated with respiratory illnesses among eligible responders and survivors.

Cancer

The World Trade Center Health Program covers numerous certified cancers associated with qualifying 9/11 exposure.

Mental Health

Post-traumatic stress disorder and other mental-health conditions are included among conditions recognized by federal programs for qualifying populations.

The Continuing Record

Medical records, epidemiological studies, survivor testimony and federal health programs remain part of the continuing historical record.
