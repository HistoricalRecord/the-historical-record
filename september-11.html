The Memorial

The people killed on September 11, 2001

2,977 people

The National September 11 Memorial & Museum identifies 2,977 people killed as a direct result of the September 11 attacks. They came from many countries, professions, families and communities.

The victims included office workers, firefighters, police officers, emergency medical personnel, airline crews, passengers, restaurant workers, maintenance workers, visitors and people who happened to be in the affected locations.

The Memorial preserves their names and the historical record surrounding their lives and deaths.

September 11, 2001: Detailed Chronology

A documented chronology from the early morning through the President's address to the nation at 8:30 p.m.

5:33 a.m. EDT

Mohamed Atta and Abdul Aziz al Omari depart Portland

Mohamed Atta and Abdul Aziz al Omari boarded a Colgan Air flight from Portland, Maine, to Boston Logan International Airport as part of the movements preceding the hijacking of American Airlines Flight 11.

Sources: 9/11 Commission, Chapter 1
6:00 a.m. EDT

Portland to Boston

Atta and al Omari traveled from Portland to Boston Logan International Airport. They were among the hijackers who would board Flight 11.

9/11 Commission
7:59 a.m. EDT

American Airlines Flight 11 departs Boston

American Airlines Flight 11 departed Boston Logan International Airport for Los Angeles with 92 people aboard.

9/11 Commission
8:14 a.m. EDT

United Airlines Flight 175 departs Boston

United Airlines Flight 175 departed Boston Logan International Airport for Los Angeles with 65 people aboard.

9/11 Commission
8:20 a.m. EDT

American Airlines Flight 77 departs Dulles

American Airlines Flight 77 departed Washington Dulles International Airport for Los Angeles with 64 people aboard.

9/11 Commission
8:24 a.m. EDT

Suspicious transmission from Flight 11

Boston Center heard a transmission from Flight 11 that indicated a hijacking was underway. Controllers began trying to establish the aircraft's position and status.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 1
8:25–8:32 a.m. EDT

FAA begins receiving reports of a hijacking

Boston Center managers began notifying the FAA chain of command that American 11 had been hijacked and was heading toward New York. The Command Center was notified at approximately 8:28.

9/11 Commission
8:42 a.m. EDT

United Airlines Flight 93 departs Newark

United Airlines Flight 93 departed Newark International Airport for San Francisco after a delay.

9/11 Commission
8:46:40 a.m. EDT

American Airlines Flight 11 strikes the North Tower

Flight 11 crashed into floors 93 through 99 of the North Tower of the World Trade Center. The impact immediately killed hundreds of people and trapped many others above the impact zone.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
8:48–8:50 a.m.

New York emergency response begins

New York City's Office of Emergency Management began activating its emergency operations structure. Fire, police, medical and other agencies began mobilizing.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
8:55 a.m.

President Bush receives initial information in Florida

President George W. Bush was preparing to visit an elementary school in Sarasota, Florida. National Security Advisor Condoleezza Rice spoke with him before he entered the classroom.

9/11 Commission hearing
9:03:11 a.m. EDT

United Airlines Flight 175 strikes the South Tower

Flight 175 struck floors 77 through 85 of the South Tower. The second impact made clear to observers that the first crash had not been an accident.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
Approximately 9:05 a.m.

President Bush is told America is under attack

Chief of Staff Andrew Card informed President Bush that a second aircraft had struck the South Tower and that America was under attack.

9/11 Commission hearing
9:20 a.m.

FAA establishes a hijacking conference

FAA headquarters established a conference involving several agencies as officials attempted to understand the unfolding attacks.

9/11 Commission
Approximately 9:25 a.m.

White House video conference begins

Richard Clarke initiated a White House video conference involving federal agencies and officials attempting to establish what was happening and how the government should respond.

9/11 Commission
9:37:46 a.m.

American Airlines Flight 77 strikes the Pentagon

Flight 77 struck the western side of the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia. The aircraft carried 64 people and 125 people inside the Pentagon were killed.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
9:37–9:40 a.m.

Emergency operations intensify in Washington

The Pentagon attack triggered emergency actions across the capital. Vice President Cheney was moved into the underground Presidential Emergency Operations Center.

9/11 Commission
9:45 a.m.

White House and federal buildings evacuated

Federal facilities in Washington were evacuated as officials attempted to protect the government from additional attacks.

9/11 Commission
9:59 a.m.

South Tower collapses

The South Tower collapsed approximately 56 minutes after it was struck by Flight 175. The collapse destroyed much of the structure and created an enormous cloud of dust and debris.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
10:03:11 a.m.

United Airlines Flight 93 crashes in Pennsylvania

Flight 93 crashed in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania. Passengers and crew had learned that other hijacked aircraft had struck targets and attempted to regain control of the plane.

9/11 Commission
10:28:25 a.m.

North Tower collapses

The North Tower collapsed 102 minutes after Flight 11 struck it. The collapse brought the immediate destruction of both Twin Towers and dramatically changed the rescue operation.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
10:30 a.m.

Air traffic restrictions expand nationwide

The FAA and federal authorities continued shutting down the national airspace system as the country responded to the possibility of additional attacks.

9/11 Commission
Late morning

Rescue and recovery operations continue

Firefighters, police officers, emergency medical personnel, construction workers, volunteers and others continued working at the World Trade Center, Pentagon and Flight 93 crash site.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 9
Afternoon

The country begins confronting the scale of the attacks

The federal government continued emergency coordination while authorities worked to establish what had happened, locate survivors, secure critical infrastructure and determine who was responsible.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 10
Late afternoon

President Bush returns toward Washington

After remaining away from Washington for much of the day for security reasons, President Bush ordered Air Force One back toward Andrews Air Force Base and then returned to the White House.

9/11 Commission, Chapter 10
7:15 p.m.

Federal officials brief the public

A White House archive records a major evening press briefing by Attorney General John Ashcroft, Secretary of Health and Human Services Tommy Thompson, Secretary of Transportation Norman Mineta and FEMA Director Joe Allbaugh.

George W. Bush White House Archives
8:30 p.m.

President Bush addresses the nation

President George W. Bush addressed the American people from the White House. He spoke about the victims, the attacks, the response, the search for those responsible and the country's determination to respond.

Full archived address 9/11 Commission
8:35 p.m.

The address ends

The White House transcript records the conclusion of the President's address at 8:35 p.m. The day continued after the speech with further meetings of the National Security Council.

White House archived transcript

The Pentagon

September 11 in Arlington, Virginia

American Airlines Flight 77

At 9:37:46 a.m., Flight 77 struck the western side of the Pentagon. The aircraft carried 64 people. Another 125 people inside the Pentagon were killed.

The response involved military personnel, firefighters, police, medical workers, federal officials and civilians who assisted colleagues and others inside the building.

Sources: 9/11 Commission Chapter 9 National Archives

Flight 93

The passengers and crew near Shanksville

The passenger response

Passengers and crew learned through telephone calls that other hijacked aircraft had struck the World Trade Center and Pentagon. They organized an attempt to regain control of their aircraft.

The aircraft crashed in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania at 10:03:11 a.m.

Sources: 9/11 Commission Chapter 9 Flight 93 National Memorial

Rescue, Recovery and Cleanup

The work continued long after September 11

The people who worked at Ground Zero

Thousands of firefighters, police officers, paramedics, construction workers, engineers, laborers, sanitation workers, volunteers and others worked at the World Trade Center site and related locations.

Their work included searching for survivors and human remains, stabilizing structures, removing debris, cleaning buildings, transporting material and supporting the recovery effort.

The work exposed many people to dust, smoke and other hazardous materials, with consequences that continued for years.

Sources: 9/11 Commission CDC World Trade Center Health Program EPA evaluation

Long-Term Health

The consequences of September 11 continued for decades

Respiratory illness

Exposure to dust and other substances at the World Trade Center and other response locations has been associated with respiratory conditions recognized by the World Trade Center Health Program.

Cancer

The WTC Health Program provides monitoring and treatment for certified cancers associated with qualifying 9/11 exposure.

Mental health

Psychological consequences affected responders, survivors, families and communities and remain part of the health history of September 11.

Federal health program

The World Trade Center Health Program provides medical monitoring and treatment to eligible responders and survivors.

WTC Health Program
Sources: CDC Victim Compensation Fund

Immigrant Workers

Workers whose contributions and experiences are part of the aftermath

Work, immigration and access to assistance

Immigrant workers participated in cleanup, construction, food service, transportation, debris removal and other forms of work connected with the recovery effort.

Immigration status could create additional barriers for some workers, including concerns about documentation, employment relationships, access to information and willingness to interact with authorities.

These experiences are documented in government investigations, academic research and oral histories.

Sources: EPA evaluation Peer-reviewed research Victim Compensation Fund

Archival Standards

How this page handles sensitive historical material

Accuracy. Facts are tied to identifiable sources. Unknown information is not filled with guesses.

Names and biographies. Names are reproduced from the Memorial's official directory. Biographical claims should be checked against reliable archival material before being added.

Photographs. Images are shown with visible credit and a link to the image record or source. Copyright and family permissions remain with the applicable rights holders.

Graphic material. Images of death, human remains or severe destruction should be included only when their historical purpose is clear and with appropriate warning and context.

Source visibility. Every major section carries its sources directly underneath the material so the archive remains readable as a single page while evidence stays visible.
