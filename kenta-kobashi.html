Kenta Kobashi

小橋 建太
The Burning Lariat. The Absolute Champion.

The Burning Lariat. The Absolute Champion.

Kenta Kobashi is one of the most celebrated professional wrestlers of his generation. From his rise through All Japan Pro Wrestling to his legendary years in Pro Wrestling NOAH, Kobashi built a career defined by championship success, extraordinary endurance, devastating strikes, unforgettable rivalries, and a determination to continue wrestling despite some of the most serious injuries and health challenges of his career.

Quick Facts

Full Name Kenta Kobashi
Japanese Name 小橋 建太
Born March 27, 1967
Professional Debut February 26, 1988
Primary Promotions All Japan Pro Wrestling and Pro Wrestling NOAH
Retirement May 11, 2013
A career built on endurance

Kobashi's career included major championship victories, legendary matches against wrestlers such as Mitsuharu Misawa, Toshiaki Kawada, Akira Taue and Stan Hansen, repeated battles with serious knee and arm injuries, and a successful return to professional wrestling after undergoing treatment for kidney cancer.

Early All Japan Years

Kenta Kobashi entered All Japan Pro Wrestling during the late 1980s. Before his official debut, he appeared as a trainee in a battle royal with Tsuyoshi Kikuchi on December 16, 1987. His official professional debut came on February 26, 1988, against Motoshi Okuma in Rittō, Shiga.

The early years were difficult. Kobashi became known for a lengthy singles losing streak, eventually reaching 63 consecutive singles defeats. Rather than preventing his rise, the streak became part of the story of his development as a wrestler. His determination and willingness to improve eventually earned him Rookie of the Year recognition.

Kobashi gradually established himself in the All Japan tag division. He won his first All Asia Tag Team Championship with Tiger Mask II, Mitsuharu Misawa, before later winning the championship with Johnny Ace.

The Super Generation Army

In 1990, Kobashi became part of the Super Generation Army alongside Mitsuharu Misawa, Toshiaki Kawada, Akira Taue and Tsuyoshi Kikuchi. The group became central to one of the most important periods in All Japan Pro Wrestling history.

Kobashi's partnership with Kikuchi became especially important. On May 25, 1992, the two defeated Can-Am Express in Sendai to win the All Asia Tag Team Championship.

Their work together became so highly regarded that their 1992 championship match was later recognized by the Wrestling Observer Newsletter as its Match of the Year.

During this period Kobashi also began developing the physical style that would become his trademark. His powerful chops, suplexes, lariats and remarkable ability to absorb punishment made him one of All Japan's most recognizable rising stars.

The Four Heavenly Kings Era

The 1990s eventually produced one of the most famous periods in Japanese professional wrestling, centered around Kobashi, Mitsuharu Misawa, Toshiaki Kawada and Akira Taue.

Kobashi's rivalry with Stan Hansen became one of the defining stories of his rise. He repeatedly challenged himself against larger and more experienced opponents while continuing to develop into one of All Japan's leading singles wrestlers.

In 1993, Kobashi recorded his first singles victory over Terry Gordy. He also participated in highly acclaimed matches against Steve Williams, including the famous Homicide Backdrop encounters.

Kobashi's 1993 match with Stan Hansen was awarded five stars by Dave Meltzer's Wrestling Observer Newsletter, further establishing his reputation as one of the world's elite wrestlers.

On December 3, 1993, Kobashi and Misawa defeated the Holy Demon Army to win the World Tag Team Championship.

The Road to the Triple Crown

Kobashi continued to rise through the All Japan singles ranks in 1994. He won his first singles match against Stan Hansen and challenged Steve Williams for the Triple Crown Heavyweight Championship at the Nippon Budokan on September 3, 1994.

One of the defining matches of Kobashi's career came on January 19, 1995, when he wrestled Toshiaki Kawada to a 60-minute draw. The match became one of the most highly regarded hour-long matches in professional wrestling history.

The following period brought serious physical problems. Kobashi suffered a significant arm injury following a chair incident involving Stan Hansen and also began dealing with major knee problems that would follow him for much of his career.

His rivalry with Mitsuharu Misawa continued to produce major singles and tag team matches, including a Triple Crown challenge against Misawa on October 25, 1995.

Triple Crown Champion

On July 24, 1996, Kobashi defeated Akira Taue to win the Triple Crown Heavyweight Championship for the first time.

Kobashi had long been influenced by Stan Hansen's Western Lariat. After winning the Triple Crown, his version became known as the Burning Lariat, one of the most recognizable finishing moves in Japanese professional wrestling.

Kobashi's rise continued through the late 1990s. In the 1997 Champion Carnival, he scored his first pinfall victory over Mitsuharu Misawa in singles competition.

Burning

Kobashi's partnership with Jun Akiyama grew during the late 1990s. The two eventually became central figures in the Burning stable, which also included Yoshinobu Kanemaru and Kentaro Shiga.

On June 12, 1998, Kobashi defeated Toshiaki Kawada to win his second Triple Crown Heavyweight Championship.

Burning also became associated with one of Kobashi's most famous moves.

The Burning Hammer

On October 24, 1998, Kobashi debuted a devastating new finishing maneuver during a tag team match with Jinsei Shinzaki against Mitsuharu Misawa and Takao Omori.

The move was influenced by the Death Valley Bomb. Fans eventually gave it the name Burning Hammer.

Why the Burning Hammer became legendary

The Burning Hammer became one of the most feared moves in professional wrestling because of how rarely Kobashi used it and because of the reputation surrounding the move.

In 1998, Kobashi and Jun Akiyama also won the World's Strongest Tag Determination League, defeating Stan Hansen and Big Van Vader in the tournament final.

The End of All Japan

Kobashi's rivalry with Vader continued into 1999. He also remained deeply involved in the ongoing rivalries involving Misawa, Kawada, Taue and the No Fear team.

In February 2000, Kobashi defeated Big Van Vader to win his third Triple Crown Heavyweight Championship. He later won the Champion Carnival in April 2000 and recorded his first televised singles victory over Mitsuharu Misawa.

The death of Giant Baba in January 1999 and the resulting changes inside All Japan eventually led to a major organizational split. Misawa became president while Kobashi served as a board director.

On June 16, 2000, Misawa announced that he was leaving All Japan. A large group of wrestlers followed him, including Kobashi. The Triple Crown Championship was vacated and the group went on to establish Pro Wrestling NOAH.

Pro Wrestling NOAH

Kobashi became one of the foundational stars of Pro Wrestling NOAH. His NOAH debut took place on August 5, 2000, when he teamed with Jun Akiyama against Mitsuharu Misawa and Akira Taue.

Unfortunately, the years of punishment Kobashi had absorbed began taking an enormous toll on his body. His knees became increasingly damaged and he underwent multiple surgeries involving his knees and elbows.

Even after repeated setbacks, Kobashi continued to return to the ring.

The Absolute Champion

On March 1, 2003, Kobashi defeated Mitsuharu Misawa to win the GHC Heavyweight Championship.

The match became one of the defining victories of Kobashi's career, with Kobashi using the Burning Hammer to defeat Misawa.

GHC Heavyweight Championship

Kobashi's reign lasted from March 1, 2003 until March 5, 2005. During the reign he successfully defended the championship 13 times over 735 days.

During this championship run Kobashi defeated a long list of major opponents, including Tamon Honda, Masahiro Chono, Bison Smith, Yuji Nagata, Yoshinari Ogawa, Takuma Sano, Takeshi Rikio, Yoshihiro Takayama, Jun Akiyama, Akira Taue, Akitoshi Saito and Mike Awesome.

Kobashi's success during 2003 and 2004 earned him Wrestling Observer Newsletter Wrestler of the Year honors.

International Recognition

Kobashi's reputation eventually reached well beyond Japan. During the 2000s he made appearances in North America and Europe, including work with Harley Race's World League Wrestling and Ring of Honor.

His American appearances included matches with wrestlers such as Samoa Joe and Homicide, introducing a new generation of international fans to the wrestler who had already become a legend in Japan.

Injuries and Cancer

Kobashi's career was repeatedly interrupted by serious injuries. His knees required multiple operations and he also suffered major problems with his arms and elbows.

In 2006, Kobashi was diagnosed with kidney cancer after doctors discovered a tumor. He underwent a laparoscopic nephrectomy and chemotherapy.

Despite the seriousness of the diagnosis, Kobashi announced that he intended to return to professional wrestling.

Return from cancer

Kobashi returned to the NOAH ring in December 2007, receiving an enormous ovation from the crowd.

The Final Years

Kobashi continued wrestling through the late 2000s despite the accumulated damage to his body.

In September 2008, he underwent emergency surgery on both arms. Doctors advised him to stop performing his famous machine-gun knife-edge chops, but Kobashi continued to use the technique.

On March 1, 2009, he returned to the Nippon Budokan and defeated Masao Inoue with the Burning Lariat.

On June 8, 2009, Kobashi won the GHC Openweight Hardcore Championship, which became the final championship of his career.

Only days later, on June 13, 2009, Mitsuharu Misawa died after suffering an injury during a match. The loss deeply affected Japanese professional wrestling and Kobashi personally.

Kobashi also made a one-night return to All Japan Pro Wrestling on August 30, 2009, marking his first All Japan match in nine years.

In December 2009, serious nerve damage to his right arm forced another extended absence from wrestling.

Kobashi returned to the ring on July 23, 2011. He also participated in the All Together charity event with Keiji Muto, where the two defeated Takashi Iizuka and Toru Yano.

Retirement

On December 9, 2012, Kobashi announced that he would retire from professional wrestling in 2013.

His retirement match was announced for the Nippon Budokan, the building that had hosted so many of the defining matches of his career.

On May 11, 2013, Kenta Kobashi wrestled his final professional match at Final Burning in Budokan.

The retirement event featured appearances and tributes from many of the most important figures in Japanese professional wrestling, including Jun Akiyama, Keiji Muto, Kensuke Sasaki, Akira Taue, Hiroshi Hase, Masahiro Chono, Toshiaki Kawada and others.

The final match

Kobashi teamed with Jun Akiyama, Keiji Muto and Kensuke Sasaki against Go Shiozaki, KENTA, Maybach Taniguchi and Yoshinobu Kanemaru.

Kobashi scored the final pinfall of his career on Yoshinobu Kanemaru with a moonsault.

Approximately 17,000 fans attended the event, which was also broadcast live in Japan and shown in movie theaters.

Legacy

Kenta Kobashi's legacy is built around far more than championship statistics. His career became a story of perseverance, rivalries, physical sacrifice and an extraordinary connection with wrestling fans.

His matches with Mitsuharu Misawa, Toshiaki Kawada, Akira Taue, Stan Hansen, Steve Williams, Vader and many others helped define one of the most influential eras in Japanese professional wrestling.

His Burning Lariat became iconic. His Burning Hammer became one of wrestling's most famous finishing moves. His long GHC Heavyweight Championship reign became one of the defining accomplishments of Pro Wrestling NOAH.

Even after serious knee injuries, arm surgeries and treatment for kidney cancer, Kobashi repeatedly returned to the ring.

His retirement in 2013 marked the end of one of the most remarkable careers in Japanese professional wrestling.
